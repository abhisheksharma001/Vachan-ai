## 11.1 The context (why this fits Vande Bharatam)
Vande Bharatam (announced 25 June 2026) is a nationwide initiative to find innovators across **36 States/UTs, 800+ districts, and multiple Indian languages**, selecting **75 finalists** for mentorship + investor engagement in Ahmedabad. Its own framing: **>80% of startup founders come from just five cities** — the goal is to reach *beyond* the hubs.

**The bottleneck isn't only idea discovery — it's communication access:** confidence, mentorship quality, regional trust, and pitch translation. A single formal-English chatbot fails most of this audience. A **tone-aware mentor** that adapts from English to Hinglish, simplifies without condescension, and preserves regional pride could materially improve participation quality.

---

## 11.2 The positioning lines (use these in the application)
- **Hook:** *"Vande Bharatam needs not only AI translation, but AI communication empathy."* Translation changes words; a tone layer changes how an agent mentors, encourages, clarifies, and challenges a founder.
- **One-liner:** *"A Tone Graph AI mentor network that helps every Indian innovator explain, refine, and pitch their idea in the language and communication style they trust — while giving Vande Bharatam a scalable, multilingual way to identify grassroots potential."*
- **Impact:** *"For a programme spanning 36 States/UTs, 800+ districts, and multiple Indian languages, tone-aware agents reduce language friction, improve application quality, and make national mentorship feel local."*
- **Defensible claim (don't overclaim):** not *"we invented persona AI"* — rather *"we built the missing middleware: a persistent, measurable tonality layer for multilingual Indian agents."*

## 11.3 The demo: three mentor agents, one shared Tone Graph
Unlike the SMB product (which clones a *user*), here the personas are **designed mentor voices** (`02` §2.7, `subject_type = designed_agent`). Three agents:

| Agent | Voice (capsule) | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Intake Mentor** | warm, encouraging, **Hinglish-first**, calm senior-founder energy | Hears a messy idea in Hindi/Hinglish, responds warmly, extracts the core idea |
| **Idea Clarifier** | patient, **Hindi-first**, simple, low-jargon | Asks culturally natural follow-up questions; fills gaps without intimidating |
| **Pitch Coach** | direct, structured, **English** but India-aware | Rewrites the idea investor-ready *without erasing regional identity* |

All three **share the founder's memory** (idea, sector, stage, constraints, language comfort) but keep **separate voices** (one persona per context — `02` §2.6). The Tone Graph records: language comfort, confidence level, preferred depth, feedback style.

---

## 11.4 The demo flow (the script to show evaluators)
```
1. Founder explains an idea in messy Hinglish/Hindi (voice or text).
2. Intake Mentor responds warmly in the founder's mix, extracts the idea.
3. Tone Graph records: language comfort, confidence, preferred depth, feedback style.
4. RAG retrieves relevant programme + startup guidance (eligibility, sector tips).
5. Clarifier asks 2–3 natural, low-jargon follow-ups.
6. Pitch Coach rewrites the idea into an investor-ready English pitch — keeping local context.
7. Next session REMEMBERS both the facts AND the tone preferences.
```

**The killer before/after (this is what makes non-technical evaluators *feel* it):** show the **same factual answer** delivered four ways — a generic chatbot, a Hindi-first mentor, a friendly Hinglish peer, and a direct investor coach. They'll immediately feel that the personality is **persistent, adaptive, and useful** — not a gimmick.
